The challenge

The work became difficult before anyone started writing.

A new request could arrive with hundreds of pages to understand. Teams had to search previous proposals, look through internal knowledge, ask experts for help, compare information, and only then begin writing.

The opportunity was not simply to make AI write faster.
The real design question was: How might AI help people reach a strong starting point without hiding the evidence they need to trust the result?

The existing workflow

Information was spread across people, files, and systems.

Search Read Compare Ask Rewrite Verify

The design opportunity

Reduce the effort of getting started, not the responsibility of finishing.

AI could bring useful information together and create a working first draft. People would still provide context, check accuracy, apply business judgment, and approve the final response.

AI opportunity

The opportunity was not “AI writes the document.”

The opportunity was to redesign the slowest part of the workflow: turning scattered company knowledge into a useful starting point that people could understand, improve, and approve.

What AI can do

Handle repeatable information work.

Retrieve

Search approved knowledge and bring relevant information into one place.

Connect

Compare information across documents and identify useful relationships.

Summarise

Turn long content into a shorter, easier-to-review starting point.

Draft

Create an editable first version based on the available evidence.

Flag gaps

Show where evidence is missing or where expert input may still be needed.

What AI cannot do

Own business judgment or accountability.

Know unstated context

AI cannot automatically understand details that were never documented or shared.

Decide what matters most

People must apply customer knowledge, business priorities, and professional judgment.

Guarantee correctness

A generated response still needs evidence, review, and expert validation.

Accept responsibility

AI cannot be accountable for commercial, legal, or business outcomes.

Approve the final response

Final approval remains a deliberate action performed by a responsible person.

The design principle became: AI accelerates the work of understanding and preparing. People remain responsible for judging and approving.

Design principles

The workflow needed to be fast, inspectable, and human-led.

01

Reduce the busy work

Help people reach a useful starting point without requiring them to search every document manually.

AI retrieves, compares, summarises, and drafts. The workflow removes repetitive effort without removing professional judgment.

02

Keep the evidence close

Important AI responses should remain connected to the information that supports them.

Sources, references, missing information, and confidence cues are available during review instead of being hidden behind another step.

03

Make review unavoidable

A polished AI draft should never look like an approved final answer.

Generated content remains editable, evidence stays visible, and approval is separated from generation as a clear human action.

From a new request to a ready-to-review response.

Trust in the workflow

Trust should be designed into the work, not added as a warning.

Trust is built through what people can see, inspect, change, and approve at each stage.

Evidence

Important AI responses remain connected to source information so users can check where an answer came from.

Editability

Generated content remains fully editable. AI output is treated as a starting point, not an instruction.

Uncertainty

Missing information and low-confidence areas are made visible instead of being hidden behind a confident-looking response.

Approval

Final approval remains a separate, intentional human action with clear accountability.

Testing and iteration

Testing showed that speed alone did not create confidence.

Proposal owners and reviewers tried finding information, generating a response, checking evidence, and approving the final output.

01

Observation

The draft looked finished too early.

People moved quickly through the generated response until they needed to understand where a specific answer came from. Source references were moved closer to the AI output.

02

Observation

Review was easy to treat as optional.

Users sometimes skimmed AI text when review appeared to be a final clean-up step. Review actions became more visible throughout the workflow.

03

Observation

People wanted a clear final checkpoint.

An editable draft and visible evidence increased confidence, but users still wanted a deliberate human approval step before publishing.

Outcome

Less time gathering information. More time improving the response.

The redesigned workflow helped teams begin with a stronger, evidence-backed first draft while keeping review and accountability visible.

Workflow outcome

Knowledge outcome

Trust outcome

Find faster. Understand better. Keep people responsible for the final decision.

Reflection

The most important design decision was not adding AI.

It was deciding where AI should help, where it should remain transparent, and where people needed to stay responsible.

This project reinforced that useful enterprise AI is not a chat box placed on top of an existing workflow. It requires redesigning how information is found, how uncertainty is communicated, and how people remain in control of important decisions.

The best AI experience did not make people disappear from the workflow. It helped them spend less time searching and more time applying their expertise.
