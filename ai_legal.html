The challenge

The work was not difficult because legal teams lacked expertise. It was difficult because the information was scattered.

Reviewers moved between contracts, folders, emails, notes, and earlier versions before they could begin reviewing. The legal decision often took less time than finding and verifying the information behind it.

01

The contract wasn't enough.

Reviewers needed emails, notes, and older versions to understand the full context.

02

Finding information took too long.

People spent too much time searching before meaningful review could begin.

03

Review cycles kept stretching.

Teams repeatedly checked and compared the same information before moving forward.

04

Important changes were easy to miss.

Long documents made small but important changes harder to notice.

Research insights

Lawyers knew how to review contracts. The challenge was feeling confident that nothing important had been missed.

Across user conversations, three needs came up repeatedly: understanding the context, checking the source, and staying in control of the final decision.

01

Too much time went into drafting

Writing and revising contracts often took attention away from higher-value legal work.

02

Review work felt repetitive

Teams repeatedly searched, checked, and compared information across long documents.

03

Changes were difficult to track

Non-standard edits and multiple versions made it harder to understand what had changed.

04

Important details could be missed

Long documents and time pressure made careful review harder to sustain.

What we learned

People did not want AI to make legal decisions for them.

They wanted help finding relevant information, understanding changes, and checking the evidence before making the final call.

Market review

Existing tools like Harvey, Spellbook, Luminance showed what AI could do. The opportunity was making those capabilities easier to understand and verify.

How research shaped the design

Research did not just validate the design. It changed what we built.

01 · What we saw

Reviewers spent too much time searching and comparing information.

02 · What we realized

Speed alone would not build trust. People needed context and evidence.

03 · What we changed

We brought relevant information, sources, and risks into the review flow.

04 · The result

A clearer review experience where AI supported judgment, not replaced it.

A decision we didn't make

We chose not to show everything at once.

We considered showing every AI finding on one screen.

During testing, that created too much to process. When a contract had many flagged items, reviewers were unsure where to begin.

So we showed the most important risks first and kept the remaining findings one click away.

The review felt simpler, more focused, and easier to move through.

What changed through testing

Testing showed us where confidence broke down.

Each iteration responded to a moment where people slowed down, felt uncertain, or needed more evidence.

Observed

People could not verify AI recommendations.

Changed

Added source references and related agreements.

Design outcome

Clearer evidence during review.

Observed

People wanted support, not automatic decisions.

Changed

Added clear review and approval checkpoints.

Design outcome

Clear ownership of the final decision.

Observed

Design outcome

Less effort switching between documents.

Observed

Changed

Design outcome

A more focused review experience.

Impact

The value was bigger than faster drafting. The review became easier to understand and trust.

By bringing information, evidence, and review controls together, the experience reduced unnecessary effort while keeping responsibility with the people making the decision.

For users

  • • Keep control of final decisions

For the business

  • • More consistent review workflows
  • • Less repeated manual effort
  • • Clearer review ownership
  • • A foundation for AI-assisted legal work

Key learning

People did not want more AI. They wanted less effort and more confidence.
