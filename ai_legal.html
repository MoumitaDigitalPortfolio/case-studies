The challenge

The work was not difficult because legal teams lacked expertise. It was difficult because the information was scattered.

As contract volumes grew, people had to move between documents, folders, emails, notes, and previous versions before they could even begin reviewing. The actual legal decision often took less time than finding and verifying the information behind it.

01

Information lived in too many places

Reviewers had to piece together the full story from contracts, emails, folders, notes, and earlier versions.

02

Finding the right clause took too long

A large part of the work was spent searching for the right clause, note, or contract version before review could begin.

03

Review cycles kept stretching

Teams repeatedly checked, compared, and rechecked the same information before they felt ready to move forward.

04

Small details were easy to miss

Under time pressure, a minor clause or change could be overlooked even when it had important consequences later.

What people told us

“ We know the answer is somewhere in here, but finding it takes too long.

“ I need one more check before I can trust this.

“ I do not want to miss a detail and find out later.

Research

We wanted to understand how legal teams worked when time was limited.

The research focused on what happened before a decision: how people found information, checked details, compared documents, and decided whether something was ready to move forward.

The experience

The workflow was designed around one simple idea: help people understand before asking them to decide.

The product moved from contract onboarding to discovery, understanding, review, and approval. At every stage, the system surfaced useful information while keeping the reviewer in charge.

01

Contract onboarding

Start with a clean, reliable contract.

Upload and validation were kept simple. The system checked the document and detected possible duplicates before review began, reducing avoidable errors later.

Why this mattered

A reliable review starts with confidence that the right document is in the system.

02

Find the right contract

Give people the context they need before they dive in.

Search, filters, and a quick contract overview helped reviewers understand what they were opening and why it mattered.

Design choice

Bring the most useful context forward instead of making people open documents one by one.

03

Understand the contract

Show what matters without hiding the source.

Key obligations, dates, terms, and possible risks were brought together in one view. People could move quickly while still tracing every important detail back to the contract.

Trust pattern

AI could summarize and point to information, but it never became the only source of truth.

04

Review what needs attention

Make important changes difficult to miss.

Risk highlights and redlining brought attention to critical clauses. Explanations helped reviewers understand why something had been flagged.

Why this mattered

Highlighting a risk is useful only when people can understand the reason behind it.

05

Human approval

AI could suggest. People still made the decision.

Suggestions were presented as part of a review flow, not as automatic next steps. Users could inspect, accept, edit, or discard changes before approval.

Core principle

The system supported judgment. It did not replace responsibility.

What changed through testing

Four rounds of feedback. Four moments where trust needed work.

The iterations were not about making the interface look more polished. Each change responded to a moment where users were unsure, slowed down, or felt they needed more evidence.

What we observed

Users could not verify AI recommendations.

What changed

Added source citations and similar agreements.

What we learned

Evidence increased confidence during review.

Impact

The impact was bigger than faster drafting. The product became a more confident way to review.

By bringing information, evidence, and review controls together, the experience helped legal teams move through contracts with less manual effort while keeping responsibility with the people making the decisions.

For users

  • • Stay in control of final decisions

For the business

  • • Less repeated manual work
  • • A scalable foundation for AI-assisted legal work

Key learning

People did not want more AI. They wanted less effort finding information and more confidence in what they were reviewing.

The most useful AI experience was not the one that took over the work. It was the one that made the right information easier to find, easier to understand, and easier to verify.
